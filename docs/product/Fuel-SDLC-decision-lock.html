DOC 01Requirements & non-functional requirements

Before choosing anything, we write down what "done" means — because you can't judge a decision without the requirement it serves. This is the contract the rest of the package is built to satisfy.

Vision & principles

Fuel is effortless, honest nutrition tracking. Three product principles, taken straight from the design doc, become engineering constraints: logging is instant and free forever (so the write path must be offline-first and never gated); honesty over false precision (so estimates are always shown as estimates and are correctable); and the habit lives outside the app (so widgets and notifications are first-class, not afterthoughts).

Functional requirements (from the design)

The mockups define the scope. Grouped by capability: onboarding (goal-first, minimal data, plan generation); logging (search, barcode scan, describe-in-words, manual, portion adjustment); the day (Today/Summary, targets, macro rings, empty state); tracking (weigh-in, trends, weekly report); retention surfaces (home-screen widgets, celebration moment, notifications); account (profile, settings, data export/delete); monetization (Fuel Pro paywall with restore/entitlements); theming (light, dark, and a themeable visual system). Each becomes a tracked feature spec in Phase docs.

The decisions worth the most words

ADR-001 · React Native + Expo client

Decision: Build one codebase in React Native with the Expo managed workflow. Why for you specifically: you need iOS+Android from one codebase (one person can't maintain two), the biggest ecosystem and training corpus on the planet (so Claude's guidance is deepest and most reliable here), Expo removes almost all native build/toolchain pain (EAS builds in the cloud — no Xcode/Gradle babysitting), and OTA updates let you fix bugs in hours without an App Store review. Alternatives & why not: Flutter is excellent but Dart has a smaller ecosystem and less AI training data, so you'd get less leverage from Claude; native SwiftUI+Compose doubles the work for a solo dev. Consequences: the two things RN doesn't do beautifully out of the box — the camera/scanner and widgets — are handled by thin native modules (ADR-018, 019), which is a known, well-documented path.

ADR-005 · Design tokens as the source of truth fidelity

Decision: No raw hex or pixel value ever appears in a component. Every color, space, radius, font size, shadow is a named token; themes (light, dark, and the eight explored directions) are just value maps. Why it's locked early: this is the single decision that determines whether "don't compromise the UI" survives to screen #40. Retrofitting tokens later is a full UI rewrite — the highest-regret reversal on the board. Consequences: dark mode stops being a feature and becomes a table you fill in; your design doc's eight directions become switchable themes, not forks.

ADR-008 · Offline-first with a sync queue trust · speed

Decision: Every log write hits a local SQLite store instantly and enqueues for background sync; the UI reads local. Why: the "logging is instant, always free" principle is impossible over the network — someone logging lunch underground must succeed offline. Consequences: you must design for conflict resolution (last-write-wins per entry is fine here) and a visible sync state. This is harder than a naive "call the API" app, which is exactly why it's decided now, not discovered later.

ADR-010 · Supabase as the backend scale · solo-ops

Decision: Managed Postgres with Auth, Storage, Edge Functions, and Realtime in one platform. Why for you: your data is deeply relational (users → days → entries → foods; trends are GROUP-BY queries), you get a real SQL database that scales via read replicas and pooling, and — critically for a solo operator — it's managed, so you're not running Postgres at 2am. It's open-source Postgres underneath, so there's an exit if you ever need it (no true lock-in). Alternatives & why not: Firebase's document model makes your trends/analytics queries awkward and locks you into Google; raw AWS is far too much ops for one person. Consequences: lean on Row-Level Security (ADR-012) as your primary authorization — it's a feature, not a workaround.

ADR-015 + ADR-016 · Food data & the AI, together the whole ballgame

These two decisions carry almost all the product's technical and trust risk, so they're locked with the most care. Food data (015): assemble, don't build — a free base of Open Food Facts (~2.8M global products) + USDA FoodData Central (authoritative whole foods), commercial APIs to patch branded/restaurant gaps, all cached into your own Postgres so a barcode hits your cache first and you only pay external APIs for the long tail. A user-correction/crowd layer (fed by scan-miss recovery) compounds over time. The AI (016): the LLM parses language into structured items and quantities only — it never invents calorie numbers. The macros come from your database, keyed to the items it identified. The evidence this is right: in independent testing, LLMs run ±16–22% mean error on calories with only 35–48% of estimates within ±10% of truth, versus ±2–5% and 95%+ for verified databases; they're also inconsistent session-to-session (±15–28%). So we use the LLM for what it's good at (understanding "two eggs and buttered toast") and the database for what it's good at (the numbers). Always show parsed items as editable chips before logging — a correctable estimate is trustworthy; a silent wrong number destroys trust.

ADR-017 · A vendor-abstracted AI gateway flexibility · cost

Decision: All AI calls go through one internal module with a provider interface — never call a vendor SDK directly from features. Why: models and prices change every few months; you want to switch providers or route common requests to a cheaper model without touching feature code. Requests carry a JSON schema (structured output) and a confidence signal that decides whether to escalate to a stronger model. Consequences: one place to add caching, cost caps, logging, and evals for the parser's accuracy over time.

ADR-020 · RevenueCat for subscriptions don't-build-this

Decision: Use RevenueCat for the Fuel Pro paywall rather than hand-rolling StoreKit/Play Billing. Why: cross-platform entitlements, receipt validation, restore-purchases, grace periods, and subscription analytics are a notorious multi-month time sink with brutal edge cases — exactly the kind of undifferentiated heavy lifting a solo founder should buy. It has first-class Expo support. Consequences: a small revenue share, well worth the months saved and bugs avoided.

ADR-029 · Internationalize from commit #1 global

Decision: Every user-facing string goes through i18next from the start; numbers, dates, and units (kcal/kJ, g/oz, kg/lb) are locale-aware; layouts are RTL-safe. Why it's locked now: "global from the start" means retrofitting i18n after hardcoding English strings across 40 screens is a miserable, error-prone rewrite. Doing it from day one costs almost nothing; deferring it is one of the classic expensive reversals.

DOC 03System architecture

The shape is deliberately simple — a solo operator's architecture should have as few moving parts as possible. Three tiers: the app, a thin serverless middle for anything with a secret, and managed data.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ MOBILE APP (React Native + Expo) │ │ UI · design tokens · Reanimated │ │ SQLite (offline) ⇄ sync queue │ └───────────────┬───────────────┬───────────┘ CRUD (RLS-guarded) │ │ secret / AI / 3rd-party ▼ ▼ ┌───────────────────┐ ┌───────────────────────┐ │ SUPABASE │ │ EDGE FUNCTIONS (Deno) │ │ Postgres + Auth │ │ AI gateway · food-API │ │ Storage · Realtime│ │ fanout · webhooks │ └─────────┬─────────┘ └───────────┬───────────┘ │ │ ▼ ▼ ┌───────────────────┐ ┌───────────────────────┐ │ Food cache (yours) │ │ AI providers · OFF · │ │ + crowd layer │ │ USDA · commercial APIs │ └───────────────────┘ └───────────────────────┘ observability: Sentry (errors) · PostHog (product) · structured logs w/ trace-id product infra: RevenueCat (billing) · Expo Notifications (push)

Trust boundary: the app never holds a third-party API key or talks to an AI provider directly. Anything with a secret or a cost goes through an Edge Function, so you can cache, rate-limit, cap spend, and rotate keys without shipping an app update. Two critical data flows get their own sequence docs in the repo: the barcode path (scan → local cache → your DB cache → edge fanout → normalize → store → return) and the describe-meal path (text → edge → AI structure extraction → DB macro lookup → editable confirmation → local write → sync).

DOC 04Data model & schema

Kept relational and boring on purpose. Row-Level Security means a user can only ever touch their own rows — a whole class of "user A saw user B's data" bugs is impossible by construction, which matters enormously when you're the only reviewer.

-- core tables (simplified) profiles id(uuid, =auth.uid) · locale · units · goal · targets{kcal,p,c,f} · created_at log_entries id · user_id → profiles · day(date) · food_id → foods · grams source(scan|describe|search|manual) · logged_at · updated_at · client_id(uuid) foods id · source · barcode? · name · brand? · per_100g{kcal,p,c,f,...} verified_by(usda|off|commercial|crowd) · locale · updated_at food_cache_misses barcode · attempts · resolved(bool) -- feeds crowd layer + coverage metrics weigh_ins user_id · day · weight_g · energy_score? entitlements user_id · tier(free|pro) · source(revenuecat) · expires_at -- synced from RevenueCat -- every user table: RLS default-deny, policy = (user_id = auth.uid()) -- trends/weekly report = GROUP BY day queries; no separate time-series system needed yet -- client_id on log_entries makes offline sync idempotent (dedupe on replay)

Migration policy: every schema change is a checked-in, reversible migration run in CI against staging before prod — never a manual console edit. Index the hot paths early (log_entries(user_id, day), foods(barcode)). Design the schema assuming millions of rows from day one; it costs nothing now and avoids a painful migration later.

DOC 05API & contracts

Two surfaces, one rule: the type is the contract. Types are generated from the database schema and shared across app and functions, so a schema change surfaces as a compile error, not a production incident — this is most of what "easy to debug" means for you.

  • CRUD goes directly through the typed Supabase client, guarded by RLS. No hand-written endpoints for simple reads/writes.
  • Edge Functions (typed, Zod-validated at the boundary) handle: the AI gateway, food-API fanout + normalization, RevenueCat webhooks, and the widget's tiny cached data endpoint.
  • Contract discipline: every function validates input and output with Zod; errors use one structured shape ({code, message, retriable}); anything a client might retry (log sync, purchases) is idempotent via client_id; external calls are rate-limited and spend-capped.

DOC 06Security, privacy & global compliance

The moment Fuel stores what someone eats and weighs, you hold sensitive personal data. "Global from the start" means three regimes apply at once. Building for them now is vastly cheaper than a retrofit — and the penalties for getting it wrong are existential for a solo founder.

The three regimes, and what each forces

RegimeWhat it forces on FuelStatus to know
GDPR (EU/EEA)Lawful basis + explicit consent, data export & erasure, data-processing records, breach notice within 72h, privacy by designIn force; the strictest baseline — build to it
CCPA/CPRA (California)Notice at collection, "do not sell/share" (you won't sell), access & delete rightsIn force
India DPDPItemized consent notice separate from ToS, easy consent withdrawal, deletion on withdrawal, verifiable parental consent for under-18s, breach report to the Data Protection Board within 72hAct 2023; Rules finalized Nov 2025; Consent-Manager framework operational Nov 13 2026; full compliance by May 13 2027 — penalties up to ₹250 crore (~$30M)
Two things to decide as product, now

India's DPDP treats everyone under 18 as a child needing verifiable parental consent — a genuine problem for a nutrition app teenagers may want. Decide your minimum age / age-gate policy up front (many consumer health apps set 18+, or 16+ with regional handling). Second, nutrition + weight data near minors edges toward eating-disorder safety territory — plan supportive-resource messaging and avoid features that could encourage unhealthy restriction. Both are cheaper as design decisions than as retrofits.

What we build to satisfy all three

  • Data minimization — the design's "only the data we need" onboarding is exactly right; every field you don't collect is one you can't leak.
  • Working DSR flows from day one — self-serve export and hard-delete account in settings. Not a v2 feature; a launch requirement.
  • Consent done properly — itemized, separate from ToS, withdrawable as easily as granted; analytics consent separate from core function.
  • Security fundamentals — TLS in transit, encryption at rest, RLS default-deny, secrets in a vault (never in the bundle — ADR-027), least-privilege service roles, Sign in with Apple/Google, short-lived tokens.
  • A lightweight threat model (STRIDE-style) reviewed before launch and after any auth/payment change; a written incident + breach-notification runbook so a 72-hour clock never catches you improvising.
  • Data-processing agreements in place with every subprocessor (Supabase, AI provider, PostHog, Sentry, RevenueCat) and a public subprocessor list.

DOC 07Quality & testing strategy

Solo, tests aren't a nicety — they're the reviewer you don't have and the safety net that lets you (and Claude) change code fast without fear. The strategy is a pragmatic pyramid, not 100%-coverage dogma.

  • Unit (many, fast): all business logic — macro math, portion conversion, sync-queue reconciliation, AI-output parsing. Vitest.
  • Contract (critical): Zod schemas double as runtime validation and tests — every edge-function and AI boundary is checked. This catches the "the shape changed" bugs that hurt most.
  • Component: key screens/states with React Native Testing Library.
  • E2E (few, high-value): Maestro flows for the money paths — onboarding, log via each method, paywall purchase+restore, export/delete.
  • AI evals (special): a fixed test set of meal descriptions with known correct parses, scored on every parser/model change so accuracy can't silently regress. This is your trust-regression guard.
  • Definition of Done: types pass, lint passes, tests green, feature behind a flag, accessibility checked, strings externalized. Enforced by CI — a PR can't merge otherwise.

DOC 08Observability & solo ops

You can't watch dashboards all day, so the system must tell you when something's wrong. Instrument for a team of one: few signals, loud alerts, clear runbooks.

  • The four golden signals to alert on: crash-free-users %, log-sync success %, barcode/AI cache-hit & success rate, and daily AI+API spend. If those four are healthy, the app is healthy.
  • Sentry for crashes/errors with source maps and session replay — you see the exact steps to a bug without a repro.
  • PostHog for product analytics, funnels, feature flags, and A/B in one tool (EU-hosted for GDPR). One event taxonomy, documented, governed — analytics rot is real.
  • Structured logs in edge functions with a trace-id threading a request across services.
  • Cost alerts wired to the two dangerous bills (§10) so a runaway AI loop pages you before it empties your account.
  • Incident runbook + status page so "the one person" has a script under pressure, including the 72-hour breach clock.

DOC 09DevOps: environments & release

  • Four environments — dev, preview (every PR gets an installable build + isolated data), staging (prod-like, migrations tested here first), production. Separate Supabase projects and keys per environment.
  • Trunk-based + feature flags — merge small and often behind flags rather than long branches. A half-built feature ships dark; you flip it on for 1% → 10% → 100%. A bad release is a toggle, not a hotfix.
  • Two release lanes: JS/UI changes ship OTA via EAS Update in minutes (no review); native changes go through store submission on a predictable cadence with staged rollout and auto-halt on a crash-rate spike.
  • Reversible everything — migrations roll back, releases roll back, flags flip off. For a solo operator, the ability to undo instantly is worth more than any single feature.
  • CI pipeline (GitHub Actions): lint → typecheck → unit/contract → build preview → E2E on the money paths. Green or it doesn't merge.

DOC 10Cost & unit economics at scale

At 1,000 users nothing here costs real money. At millions, two line items dominate — and both are controlled by design decisions already locked, not by luck.

  1. AI "describe meal" calls — uncapped, they scale with engaged users and can dwarf everything. Controlled by caching repeated phrasings, routing common cases to a cheaper model (ADR-017), and making unlimited AI logging a Fuel Pro perk — so your heaviest cost users are your paying users.
  2. Commercial food-API calls — ruinous if you proxy every lookup, trivial if you cache into your own DB and pay only for the long tail (ADR-015). This is why the cache is built in week one, not as a later optimization.

Everything else — Postgres, storage, bandwidth, auth, Sentry, PostHog — scales gently and predictably on managed tiers. The paywall is not just revenue; it's the mechanism that keeps unit economics sane. Model this in a simple sheet (cost per active user vs. Pro conversion) and watch the daily-spend alert.

DOC 12Phase plan & exit criteria

Sequenced to prove the risky things early and to give a solo builder clear "done" lines instead of an endless slog. Since you chose quality over speed, each phase ends only when its exit criteria are met — that's the discipline that keeps "production-grade" honest. No fixed weeks (you set the pace with Claude); the order is what matters.

PHASE 0 — Foundationsthe machine that builds everything

Monorepo, TypeScript strict, design tokens + the ~15 core components in Storybook, Supabase schema with RLS, auth, i18n scaffolding, CI with preview builds, Sentry + PostHog wired, CLAUDE.md + ADR folder seeded from this package.

Exit: a signed-in user can see an empty themed Today screen on both platforms; a PR triggers a full green CI run and an installable preview build.

PHASE 1 — The core looptrack a day, instantly

Goal-first onboarding + plan, Today/Summary, manual + search logging, offline-first store & sync, food-DB cache seeded with Open Food Facts + USDA, working data export/delete.

Exit: a real person tracks a full day offline and online, it feels instant, and their data survives a reinstall. Private beta (TestFlight / Play internal).

PHASE 2 — The magic (highest risk first)scan & describe

Barcode scan (native module) + scan-miss recovery, describe-meal AI with editable-chip confirmation and the eval suite, celebration moment. Meets messy real-world input here, not at launch.

Exit: scan-to-logged under the target, AI parse accuracy passing the eval bar, cache-hit metrics healthy, cost per log within model.

PHASE 3 — Habit & retentionthe app without opening the app

Home-screen widgets (both platforms), weigh-in, trends, weekly report, dark mode, notifications.

Exit: a user can log & check progress from a widget without opening the app; dark mode is complete via tokens.

PHASE 4 — Business & compliance hardeningready to charge & to be trusted

Fuel Pro paywall (RevenueCat), profile/settings, full consent + age-gate flows, accessibility + design-system audits, threat model + breach runbook signed off, localization for launch locales.

Exit: purchase+restore works cross-platform; a WCAG AA pass; all DSR flows verified; compliance checklist complete.

PHASE 5 — Launch & iteratestaged, measured, reversible

Staged public rollout, A/B on paywall & onboarding & the visual directions, monitoring the four golden signals, feedback loop into the backlog.

Exit: crash-free-users and sync-success above target in production; iteration cadence established; nothing on fire.
